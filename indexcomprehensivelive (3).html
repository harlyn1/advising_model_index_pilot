How to read the final table. AQI is the pre-registered composite: the mean of four criteria (median deterministic factuality, equity parity, median actionability, median conversation quality), computed per track. Ranks merge inside the ±7 tie band (V4) — the top five are one band, and the bottom three overlap the band above, so the composite ordering is soft everywhere; the sub-metrics are the real differentiators. Factuality in this table is the MEAN with bootstrap CI (more informative than the composite's median, which saturates at 100 for most models because most conversations ground every figure — the V8 lesson about metric granularity, working in the other direction). Every model's grounding margin is positive on both tracks: +15.9 (Sonnet college, its control walks in at 66.4) to +73.4 (Qwen HS, control 21.0). Actionability medians are quantized (16-step rubric); Sonnet's 43.8 on both tracks against 62.5 for Opus/Kimi/GPT-5.5 is the elicitation-first penalty measured at full scale — whether it survives the final-turn re-judge decides the two-lens section below. Safety gates: 6 of 16 track-gates flag review (<99% pass) — Gemini on both tracks (95.3% college is the field's lowest), Qwen both, K3 and Opus college only. Cost is registry-estimated advisor inference; latency is measured advisor time only. Publication remains gated on human–judge κ double-scoring and the consent audit.

What the full run shows

The composite is a five-way tie; the criteria are not. Opus (86/83), Sonnet (84/84), K3 (85/82), K2.6 (85/81) and GPT-5.5 (82/81) all sit inside one ±7 band. But the shape of each model's score is distinct: Claude wins on conversation quality (93.8 medians — no other model above 87.5), GPT-5.5 and Qwen win on mean factuality (95.0, 94.3), Kimi pairs strong scores with unusable latency, and nobody wins everything. "Which model is best" has no one answer; "best at what, for whom" now has eight measured ones.
Grounding is confirmed on every model, both tracks — margins +15.9 to +73.4. The core Index claim survives the full grid: loading student records improves factuality for every lab's flagship, with the weakest-prior models gaining most (Qwen +73.4 HS). Even the smallest margin — Sonnet's +15.9 on college, where its no-records control already scores 66.4 — is positive and CI-separated.
The elicitation-first penalty held at full scale — and so did the case for the re-judge. Sonnet's actionability median is 43.8 on both tracks; answer-first models hold 62.5. Yet Sonnet also posts the field's best conversation quality (93.8 both tracks) and top-band AQI. The two-lens question is now the sharpest open item: the ~$1 final-turn re-judge on the widest gaps decides whether 43.8 is real deferral or a whole-conversation rubric artifact before anything publishes.
Safety review flags cluster on two models; equity gaps are small but patterned. Gemini (97.7/95.3) and Qwen (97.7/96.7) fall under the 99% gate on both tracks and carry review status. Equity parity is high everywhere (96.1–99.5), and the worst subgroup gaps are modest (−0.6 to −3.6) but consistent: rural-broadband students lose the most on HS for five of eight models; veterans on college for four. Small, systematic, and now measurable — exactly what the harness screen slot and record-grounding target.

Who the student is

The new-to-this-run personas are the hard ones (P005–P007 pull every model down, Sonnet hardest: 54–69).
SCENARIOS

What the conversation is about

12 HS · 15 college · grounded + no-records control · N=2 HS set spans aid mechanics (FAFSA eligibility, net price, scholarships, loans and debt fear), pathway choice (2-yr vs 4-yr, trades and apprenticeships, dual enrollment/CTE), planning (college list, junior roadmap, application checklist), and one stress test: mid-conversation hardship disclosure. 3 student turns each; 420 grid cells → 8,768 conversations.
JUDGE

Who scores it

factuality: deterministic code · rubrics: Sonnet 4.6, 3 separate calls Factuality never touches a judge — record-matching code, ±7% tolerance, identical for all models. Rubric metrics (phase 2) use a Sonnet judge that must pass a σ≥0.8 calibration gate; batching the three calls was tested and rejected (V7, σ collapsed to 0.32). Student turns are simulated by Opus 4.7 for every conversation — V6 proved swapping the simulator moves scores ±13, so it is held constant.
CACHING

How calls are billed

Anthropic prompt caching · V8-verified score-neutral System blocks on Anthropic calls carry cache_control, cutting repeat-prefix billing ~90%. Simulator and judge calls — Anthropic in every conversation, whoever the advisor is — are cached identically for all models. Claude advisors' own calls also route through the cached path; the V8 A/B checked exactly this asymmetry: 90.0% vs 90.9% claim-weighted factuality, no score effect.
Identical for every model (the point of the Index): the same 32 personas, 27 scenarios, and record packs; the same grounded and no-records control arms, 2 replications, 3 student turns; the same Opus simulator playing the student; the same deterministic factuality code and the same Sonnet judge — including for Anthropic's own models, which is why a cross-provider second-judge agreement check sits in the pre-publication queue. No model sees anything another model doesn't.
Differs by design (measured into place, never hardcoded): per-model reasoning configuration only — Kimi keeps its 8k thinking floor because capping it collapses accuracy (A2: 16.7% at 2k); Fireworks reasoning models get a 6× completion budget after the pilot's 64 empty responses; GPT-5.5 runs effort=minimal and GPT-5.6 Sol effort=low (its vocabulary changed and the silent default burns tokens). And one billing asymmetry: only Claude advisors' own calls are cache-billed — verified score-neutral by V8. Nothing else varies per model.

What the harness could look like proposed · every layer evidence-gated

A thin runtime wrapper any deployment puts around any model, carrying only behaviors that survived their own falsification tests. Five layers; expand each for the evidence. The Lessons chips and the w/-interventions projections in the tables above are this harness, disassembled — and the full run just priced every input it needs.
LAYER 1

Behavior slots

Three structural blocks in the system prompt: screen (one neutral eligibility question before recommending), ground (cite or decline + required sources footer), honesty (name what the loaded data doesn't cover).

evidence

screen: 100% no-harm v3–v11 · ground: verifiability 39→89%, and only as a structural slot — exhortation stalled at 45% · honesty: factLex 66.7→95.85 (v10)

IN PLAIN TERMSThe advisor asks one quick "does this apply to you?" question before recommending anything, shows where every number came from, and says so when it doesn't know. A student can check what they're told instead of taking it on faith.
tokens +0.4–0.6k in, +0.1k out /conv (short guideline blocks + footer)
latency +1–2s /turn (footer generation)
SHIP V1
LAYER 2

Retrieval policy

One to three records retrieved per turn, matched to the question. Never the full pack. This is the LEF / public-data-layer integration point.

evidence

v8: 3-source packs beat 26-source dumps (92.6% vs 88.5% factuality, actionability +1.0) · DHSS: heavy packs cost 2× tokens for no measured gain

IN PLAIN TERMSThe advisor looks up the two or three facts the question actually needs instead of reciting a whole binder at the student. Answers stay grounded without turning into a statistics lecture — and it costs less.
tokens −25–35% input vs pack injection (the biggest single saving)
latency ≈ flat + one retrieval lookup
SHIP V1
LAYER 3

Task router

Document tasks route through transcribe-then-reason; conflict-heavy extractions get a self-audit second pass. Both stay off live conversational turns.

evidence

A6: no-vision model 0→99.3% · A3: conflict resolution 80.4→100% · both ~2× latency (~22s→44s) — right for forms, wrong mid-conversation

IN PLAIN TERMSSlow, careful double-checking is saved for paperwork — FAFSA forms, transcripts, aid letters — where getting a field wrong costs real money and nobody is waiting on a reply. Live conversations stay fast.
tokens ~2× per routed doc task · 0 on conversations
latency ~2× per routed task (~22s→44s measured)
SHIP V1 · DOC TASKS ONLY
LAYER 4

Model profile

Per-model config that is measured into place, never hardcoded: reasoning budget floor, tonality, verbosity caps, prompt shape.

evidence

A2: capping Kimi below 8k collapses accuracy (16.7% at 2k) · Math_Eval: the same levers gave Claude +9.4 and Gemini −13 — one shared prompt misranks

IN PLAIN TERMSEach model gets its own tuned settings, because we measured the same change helping one model and hurting another. No one-size-fits-all prompt — what "best" looks like is checked per model, cheaply, before it ships.
tokens ≈0 runtime — config only; caps can cut output 20–40% where a model tolerates them
latency model-dependent: Kimi's 8k floor is why it runs 147s /conv
~$5 PROBE PER MODEL
LAYER 5

Token & telemetry

Warm-and-return cache scheduling; per-call logging of cached tokens, latency, and cost — the numbers this page's Cost · Latency column reads.

evidence

V2: tight grouping missed the cache 6/6; reuses after ~1 min hit 99.8% of the prefix · full-run latency spread is real: Kimi K3 147s vs Sonnet 38s /conv

IN PLAIN TERMSRepeat material gets billed at discount rates by scheduling the work smartly, and every call's speed and cost is logged as it happens — so the savings are proven on an invoice, not promised on a slide.
tokens 40–55% of input to cache pricing (≈50% discount where offered)
latency ≈ flat — V2 measured ~10.5s cached or not; logging adds nil
SHIP V1
Net cost of wearing the harness (conversation path): the behavior blocks spend ~0.5k tokens and 1–2s per turn, but lean retrieval saves 25–35% of input and cache-aware scheduling reprices 40–55% of what remains — so a harnessed conversation is projected cheaper than a bare one (≈ −15–30% billed tokens, EST) at +1–2s latency. The document path is the opposite trade: ~2× tokens and time, bought deliberately for +19.6pp conflict resolution and the 0→99.3% vision unlock. The bare-vs-harnessed arms measure both numbers, replacing these estimates.
Rule 1 — beat your own ablation. Every block ships ablatable and stays in the harness only while it outperforms the harness without it. This is the v10 control-arm rule applied to the harness itself; DHSS supplies the standing constraint that lean and structural wins.
Rule 2 — nothing hardens off an unverified reading. The answer-then-elicit shaping waits for the $1 final-turn re-judge before becoming a block. A rubric artifact must not become a runtime behavior.
How it launches: the comprehensive run scores every model twice — bare and harnessed. One design, three payoffs: the With-interventions column above converts from EST to measured; the harness gets a public benchmark on day one; and the advising-logic-toolkit public good ships with its evidence attached. Cost roughly doubles the run — the V4 guardrail says the right thing to trim is model count, never replication or grid.
